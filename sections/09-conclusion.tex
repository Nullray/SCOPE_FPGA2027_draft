\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\sys makes the peripheral subsystem visible to an FPGA-hosted DUT a software
decision. Its method combines device-presentation--backend-implementation
decoupling with control-semantics--data-path decoupling, and constrains the
split with configuration--route, submission--DMA, and data--completion
coherence. Virtualization provides a user-configured device view while physical
backends retain execution
of the exposed operations. This combination trades mediation cost and altered
timing for configurability, with fidelity bounded by the supported semantics.
The prototype evidence shows that a standard Linux stack can
enumerate and use real NVMe and Ethernet backends through a generic FPGA
interface, while the measured control and data results expose the current
performance envelope. A complete NVMe+NIC concurrency run, fair direct
baselines, and the 1/2/4/8/13-slot synthesis sweep remain necessary before
making stronger claims about composition scalability.
