\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\sys extends a peripheral-borrowing substrate by presenting a logical
PCIe view selected in software and consumed by native kernel drivers. Logical presentation is
decoupled from physical attachment, and control mediation from payload
movement. The method joins their state owners through configuration
acknowledgment, physical submission, and completion publication, with explicit
memory-visibility assumptions. Virtualization makes the supported view and
compatible binding configurable, while physical devices retain execution.
The resulting tradeoff is mediation cost and altered timing in exchange for
configurability, with fidelity bounded by the exposed semantics.
The prototype evidence shows that a standard Linux stack can
enumerate and use real NVMe and Ethernet devices through a generic FPGA
interface in separate endpoint configurations. Prior-platform measurements
characterize the inherited routing path, while data-path performance remains provisional
pending measurement reconciliation. Ordering validation, an NVMe+NIC concurrency
run, matched direct baselines, and the 1/2/4/8/13-slot synthesis sweep remain
necessary before stronger correctness, performance, and scaling claims.
